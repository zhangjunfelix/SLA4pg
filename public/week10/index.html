    Week 10 — Syntax–Discourse & Semantics–Pragmatics Interfaces #

    Anchor text: Slabakova (2016), Second Language Acquisition, Chapter 11.

    ⭐ Overview (for preview) #

    • How information structure (topic/focus) and prosody/word order interact, and why mappings differ across languages.
    • Why pronoun interpretation (null vs. overt, topic continuity/shift) and anaphora resolution are challenging external-interface phenomena.
    • How pragmatic inferences (e.g., scalar implicatures) arise and why they can be delayed or optional in L2 processing.

    🎯 Learning goals #

    By the end of this week you should be able to:

    1. Define topic, focus, and givenness, and explain how languages encode them (prosody, particles, word order, clefts).
    2. Describe how subject expression and pronoun resolution depend on syntactic and discourse cues, and derive L1-specific predictions.
    3. Explain scalar implicature computation (e.g., some → not all) and state conditions that favor or suppress it.
    4. Design a compact contexted task that elicits interface-sensitive judgments or online processing signatures.

    📖 Reading (do before Session 1) #

    • Required: Slabakova (2016), Ch. 11 (focus: topic/focus & prosody/word order; overt vs. null pronouns and discourse; scalar implicatures and other pragmatic inferences).
    • Optional: Review Ch. 3 (processing) for methods used in online pragmatics, and Ch. 8–10 for connections to syntax and semantics.

    Guiding questions:

    • When does a language mark focus with prosody alone vs. with syntax (word order/clefts) or particles (e.g., only/even)?
    • In null-subject languages, when is an overt pronoun preferred, and what does that mean for L2 learners’ choices?
    • What contexts trigger a scalar implicature, and how can we encourage/expose it experimentally?

    🧠 Key terms #

    information structure; topic; focus; givenness; contrastive focus; clefting; focus particles; prosody/intonation; subject expression; null vs. overt pronoun; anaphora resolution; discourse prominence; scalar implicature; implicature cancellation; contexted acceptability; discourse completion; TVJT; visual-world eye-tracking.

    🔍 Core ideas #

    • External interfaces are fragile: Mappings between grammar and discourse/pragmatics (topic continuity, focus interpretation, implicatures) show optional or delayed behavior in L2, even when core syntax/semantics are in place.
    • Encoding focus/topic varies by language: English often relies on prosody and clefts; many languages also use word order or particles. L2 learners must learn which cue does the heavy lifting.
    • Subject expression & pronouns: In null-subject languages, null often signals topic continuity; overt can mark topic shift/emphasis. L2 learners may overuse overt pronouns or omit required overt subjects depending on L1.
    • Scalar implicatures (SIs): Inferences like some → not all are context-sensitive and can be resource-demanding; L2ers often compute SIs less or later unless context licenses them strongly.

    🗂️ Mini-lecture outline (Session 1, 45 min) #

    1) Information structure: topic, focus, and encoding strategies #

    • Key notes:
      • Topic = what the sentence is about; focus = new/contrastive information.
      • Languages differ: prosodic focus (pitch accent) vs. syntactic (clefts, fronting) vs. particles (only/even).
      • Prediction: L2ers transfer L1-preferred cue (prosody/syntax/particle) → early mis-matches in L2.
    • Class spark: Provide 4 short contexts; students pick among plain SVO, cleft (It is X that…), or focus particle (only X) for best fit.

    2) Pronoun interpretation & subject expression at the discourse interface #

    • Key notes:
      • Null vs. overt subject pronoun choice tracks topic continuity vs. shift in many pro-drop languages.
      • L2 predictions: From a pro-drop L1 → English: omission errors; From a non-pro-drop L1 → Spanish/Italian: overuse of overt pronouns in topic-continuity contexts.
    • Micro-demo: Two-sentence mini-discourses that force continuity vs. shift; learners choose null/overt (or pronoun vs. name) and justify.

    3) Scalar implicatures and pragmatic computation #

    • Key notes:
      • SIs arise when a stronger alternative exists (all > some); speakers choose the weaker term to implicate the stronger is false.
      • Triggered by context and task; can be cancelled (Some, maybe all).
      • Prediction: L2ers accept literal readings more; SI rates go up with supportive contexts and time.
    • Think–pair–share: Craft a one-line context that forces an SI for some; then write an explicit cancellation continuation.

    Pacing: ~12 minutes per subtopic + ~9 minutes guided discussion.

    🧪 Methods micro-lab (Session 2, 45 min) #

    Goal: Build a contexted test for one interface phenomenon.

    Choose ONE and complete the template.

    • A. Topic/focus mapping

      • Materials: Contexts that require contrastive focus vs. broad focus.
      • Task: Contexted acceptability comparing plain SVO vs. cleft vs. focus particle; optional prosody track (audio with/without focal accent).
      • Prediction: L2ers overuse the L1-preferred device and show lower acceptability when the L2-preferred cue is missing.

    • B. Pronoun resolution / subject expression

      • Materials: Two-sentence discourses manipulating topic continuity vs. shift.
      • Task: Discourse completion (choose form: null/overt/name) + visual-world eye-tracking (pronoun resolution to antecedent).
      • Prediction: From non-pro-drop L1 → pro-drop L2: overuse overt subjects in continuity; eye-tracking shows delayed fixation to discourse-topical referent for null pronouns.

    • C. Scalar implicature

      • Materials: Contexts biasing not-all vs. allowing literal truth.
      • Task: TVJT (true/false/“infelicitous”) + optional self-paced reading at the implicature trigger and continuation.
      • Prediction: Lower SI endorsement overall; increased SI with supportive contexts or slower tasks.

    Deliverable: 3-line prereg—hypothesis, primary DV (acceptability, choice, RTs/fixations), and falsifier.

    🧩 In-class application (Session 3, 45 min) #

    Case vignette

    • Group A (English L1 → Spanish L2): frequent overt subjects in topic-continuity contexts; near-native case marking elsewhere.
    • Group B (Mandarin L1 → English L2): prefers clefts or focus particles over prosodic focus; literal bias on some.

    Questions:

    1. Which patterns indicate interface mapping issues vs. processing preferences?
    2. What context manipulation would make the target interpretation diagnostic (e.g., add explicit contrast/topic tracking)?
    3. Propose one instructional task per group (e.g., discourse cards guiding pronoun choice; prosody training with minimal pairs; SI drills using alternatives lists).

    📝 Weekly analytical note (due before Week 11) #

    In ~300 words, pick one domain (topic/focus, pronoun interpretation, or scalar implicature). (a) State a claim with an L1-based prediction, (b) design one contexted task with a clear DV, and (c) specify a result that would falsify your preferred account (e.g., native-like choices offline but delayed online).
