    Week 12 — Teaching Implications & the Bottleneck Hypothesis #

    Anchor text: Slabakova (2016), Second Language Acquisition, Chapter 13.

    ⭐ Overview (for preview) #

    • The Bottleneck Hypothesis (BH): why functional morphology (features and their exponents) constitutes the main learning bottleneck in L2, compared to syntax/semantics.
    • Translating theory to pedagogy: which targets are “hard vs. easy,” how to sequence input, and how to design focus-on-form activities that address the bottleneck.
    • Evaluating interventions with compact pre/post designs and diagnostics that align with the hypothesized locus of difficulty.

    🎯 Learning goals #

    By the end of this week you should be able to:

    1. State the Bottleneck Hypothesis, its main predictions, and competing explanations (e.g., processing-only accounts).
    2. Identify target properties (e.g., agreement, tense/aspect, articles, gender/case) where BH predicts persistent vulnerability.
    3. Design a theory-aligned intervention (input enhancement, processing instruction, or output practice) and specify measures that test the right locus.
    4. Draft a feasible evaluation plan (pre/post contrasts; competence and processing DVs) with an explicit falsifier.

    📖 Reading (do before Session 1) #

    • Required: Slabakova (2016), Ch. 13 (Bottleneck Hypothesis, “what is hard/easy,” instructional implications, focus on form).
    • Optional: Skim Ch. 7 (morphology) and Ch. 12 (methods) for links to diagnosis and evaluation.

    Guiding questions:

    • If morphology is the bottleneck, what kind of evidence and which outcomes (offline/online) would confirm this?
    • How could an instructor sequence form–meaning–use to mitigate the bottleneck while keeping fluency tasks intact?

    🧠 Key terms #

    Bottleneck Hypothesis (BH); functional morphology; feature bundling; form–meaning mapping; input enhancement; processing instruction; task-based focus on form; explicit vs. implicit knowledge; diagnostic evidence; pre/post design; near/far transfer; generalization.

    🔍 Core ideas #

    • BH claim: L2 learners struggle most with functional morphology (features, exponents, concord); syntax and core compositional semantics are comparatively less problematic once features are available.
    • Why morphology? Low perceptual salience, complex feature bundling, cross-language re-mapping (articles, gender/case, agreement), and distributional sparsity.
    • Pedagogical corollaries:
      • Target selection: prioritize morphology–meaning pairs with weak surface cues (e.g., article–definiteness, agreement in 3sg present, gender concord).
      • Sequencing: progress from noticing/processing (input) → guided output (mapping practice) → free use (communication with built-in diagnostic triggers).
      • Assessment: pair competence (contexted AJT/TVJT) with processing signatures (SPR/eye-tracking timing, or timed GJT) to see if the bottleneck is alleviated.

    🗂️ Mini-lecture outline (Session 1, 45 min) #

    1) The Bottleneck Hypothesis: claims & predictions #

    • Key notes:
      • Morphology carries crucial grammatical features; errors show selective optionality even with good syntax/semantics.
      • BH predicts durable challenges for agreement, tense/aspect, articles, gender/case; improvement follows when learners get diagnostic input plus practice in feature mapping.
    • Class spark: Quick vote: which one morphological domain (in your L2) felt most resistant to instruction?

    2) Competing/compatible explanations #

    • Key notes:
      • Processing-only accounts: learners have the features but fail under time pressure.
      • Prosodic/mapping accounts: realization depends on position/prosody; improve prosody to improve realization.
      • Feature Reassembly: true locus is representation (re-bundling features for new exponents).
      • Takeaway: instruction should test where the problem lies (representation vs. realization vs. time pressure).
    • Class compare: Map each explanation to a distinct prediction for comprehension vs. production vs. timing.

    3) From theory to teaching design #

    • Key notes:
      • Input enhancement (salience, contrast sets, textual/aural cues) → better noticing of form–meaning.
      • Processing instruction (meaning-first interpretation tasks) → forces learners to use the form to get meaning right.
      • Output cycles (scaffolded → communicative) → stabilize exponent selection and agreement/concord under load.
      • Diagnostics: always embed a minimal discriminator (e.g., in/for an hour for telicity; article choice in novel-specific contexts; verb–adverb placement).
    • Template reveal: The intervention canvas you’ll use in Session 2.

    Pacing: ~12 min per subtopic + ~9 min discussion.

    🧪 Methods micro-lab (Session 2, 45 min) #

    Goal: Design a theory-aligned micro-intervention that targets a morphological bottleneck and can be evaluated in one week.

    Choose ONE target and complete the template.

    • A. English 3sg -s (subject–verb agreement)

      • Noticing & processing: Input enhancement in short dialogues + forced-choice interpretation where the correct referent depends on -s.
      • Guided output: Timed sentence-building with alternating singular/plural subjects and adverb placement distractors.
      • Measures: Contexted AJT (competence) + timed GJT or mini-SPR at the verb region (processing).
      • Falsifier: No gain in verb-region timing or production accuracy despite competence gains.

    • B. English articles (definiteness/specificity)

      • Noticing & processing: Picture–context cards manipulating unique/familiar vs. novel-specific; learners must pick the/a/∅ to get the referent right.
      • Guided output: Short info-gap tasks where only the correct article resolves reference.
      • Measures: TVJT with discourse contexts + timed GJT; look for improved novel-specific discrimination.
      • Falsifier: Persistent overuse of the in novel-specific contexts under both timed and untimed conditions.

    • C. Gender concord in Spanish (det–adj–noun)

      • Noticing & processing: Color-coded feature highlighting across D–A–N; minimal pairs differing in number/gender.
      • Guided output: Rapid “build-a-NP” drills under time; then short narratives that force agreement.
      • Measures: Agreement elicitation accuracy + priming for feature match; check for reduced feature-conflict errors.
      • Falsifier: No reduction in mismatched concord errors or priming gains.

    Deliverable (in class): 6 bullets—Target, Input/Processing step, Output step, Minimal discriminator, DVs, Falsifier.

    🧩 In-class application (Session 3, 45 min) #

    Case vignettes

    • Group A (L1 Mandarin → L2 English): high comprehension of agreement contrasts; persistent 3sg -s omissions under time pressure.
    • Group B (L1 Russian → L2 English): stable telicity with measure phrases; ongoing article overuse of the in novel-specific contexts.
    • Group C (L1 English → L2 Spanish): good noun gender knowledge; adjective agreement mismatches in rapid production.

    Questions:

    1. For each group, which locus best fits (representation vs. realization vs. timing)?
    2. Which intervention component (input enhancement, processing instruction, or output cycle) is the priority, and why?
    3. Specify one pre/post DV that would show genuine bottleneck relief (not just metalinguistic gains).
    4. How would you test generalization (near transfer → same structure; far transfer → new lexical items/contexts)?

    1. Give one instructional prediction if BH is correct for agreement.
    1. Name a minimal discriminator that targets article semantics.
    1. What pattern would favor a processing-only account over BH?
    1. One way to show generalization beyond trained tokens.

    📝 Weekly analytical note (final, due with proposal) #

    In 300–400 words, write the Intervention & Evaluation section of your proposal:
    (a) Target & rationale (why it’s a bottleneck),
    (b) Instructional sequence (input → processing → output),
    (c) Diagnostics & DVs (competence + processing),
    (d) Pre/post design (materials, counterbalancing),
    (e) Falsifier & generalization plan.
