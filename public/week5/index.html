    Week 5 — Comparing Acquisition Contexts: 2L1, Adult L2, Heritage & Attrition #

    Anchor text: Slabakova (2016), Second Language Acquisition, Ch. 5–6 (selected).

    ⭐ Overview (for preview) #

    • How acquisition context (simultaneous bilingualism/2L1, sequential/Adult L2, Heritage, Attrition) shapes the path and outcomes of acquisition.
    • What transfers, what restructures, and how input timing/amount/quality and dominance shift predictions.
    • Practical takeaways for designing studies and targeted instruction in each group.

    🎯 Learning goals #

    By the end of this week you should be able to:

    1. Distinguish 2L1, Adult L2, Heritage, and Attrition profiles and state group-specific predictions for morphology, syntax, and interfaces.
    2. Explain how input timing/quality and language dominance mediate similarities/differences across groups.
    3. Evaluate whether a pattern reflects incomplete acquisition, attrition, or processing pressure.
    4. Sketch a controlled comparison (groups, property, task, covariates) that can adjudicate between explanations.

    📖 Reading (do before Session 1) #

    • Required: Slabakova (2016), Ch. 5–6 (selected)
      Focus your notes on: (i) 2L1 vs. Adult L2 contrasts, (ii) Heritage acquisition profiles and dominance, (iii) Attrition vs. incomplete acquisition, (iv) Illustrative morpho-syntactic phenomena.
    • Optional: Revisit Ch. 3–4 highlights (processing, age/input) for links to Week 5.

    Guiding questions:

    • Which properties are most likely to differ across contexts (e.g., functional morphology, clitic placement, subject expression, gender/CASE, articles/definiteness), and why?
    • How would you tell incomplete acquisition apart from attrition using design and measures?

    🧠 Key terms #

    2L1 / simultaneous bilingualism; sequential bilingualism; Adult L2; Heritage language; dominance; input timing/quality; incomplete acquisition; attrition; restructuring; crosslinguistic influence; stabilization; ultimate attainment; interfaces (syntax–semantics/pragmatics).

    🔍 Core ideas #

    • Context matters: 2L1 receives early, rich, balanced input in both languages; Adult L2 begins late, often classroom-heavy; Heritage learners have early naturalistic input but later reduced input and dominance shift; Attriters show loss/weakening tied to reduced use.
    • Predicted strengths/weaknesses:
      • 2L1: native-like developmental path; occasional crosslinguistic influence but robust end-state when input is sufficient.
      • Adult L2: vulnerabilities in functional morphology and some interfaces; stronger role for transfer and processing pressure.
      • Heritage: strong phonology/lexicon; selective vulnerabilities in morphology and discourse-conditioned syntax (e.g., subject expression, clitics) due to input profile.
      • Attrition: erosion in low-frequency/late-acquired morpho-syntax and discourse mapping, especially under limited use.
    • Diagnosing source: Distinguish incomplete acquisition (never fully robust) vs. attrition (previously acquired, later weakened) via age-of-onset, input history, use, and change over time.

    🗂️ Mini-lecture outline (Session 1, 45 min) #

    1) Mapping the contexts: who acquires what, when? #

    • Key notes: 2L1 vs. Adult L2 vs. Heritage vs. Attrition; typical AoO, input type, domains of strength/weakness; role of dominance.
    • Class spark: Quick poll—Which group would you expect to outperform in phonology, functional morphology, interface pragmatics, and processing speed? Why?

    2) Group-specific predictions by domain #

    • Key notes:
      • Functional morphology (agreement, tense/aspect, gender/CASE): Adult L2 & Heritage often show selective instability; 2L1 less so with balanced input.
      • Syntax (movement, clitic placement): Adult L2 may show transfer-shaped errors; Heritage may show optional forms tied to discourse routines.
      • Interfaces (syntax–semantics/pragmatics): Heritage and Adult L2 can lag where discourse cues are sparse in input.
    • Example: Subject expression (null vs. overt) and clitics in Romance; articles/definiteness in English; gender agreement in Spanish.

    3) Incomplete acquisition vs. attrition: design and evidence #

    • Key notes:
      • Incomplete acquisition: property never stabilized (traceable to early input profile).
      • Attrition: previously stabilized but erodes with reduced use.
      • Use history, longitudinal or age-stratified designs, and task contrasts (online vs. offline) to diagnose.
    • Think–pair–share: For “gender agreement in Heritage Spanish,” list two patterns that would imply incomplete acquisition vs. attrition.

    🧪 Methods micro-lab (Session 2, 45 min) #

    Goal: Build a compact study that cleanly compares two contexts on one property.

    1. Choose a property (one only):

      • (A) Gender agreement (Spanish); (B) Object clitic placement (Romance); (C) Subject expression (null vs. overt); (D) English article definiteness.

    2. Pick two groups (match on age now, SES, and education where possible):

      • 2L1 vs. Adult L2 • 2L1 vs. Heritage • Heritage vs. Adult L2 • Heritage vs. Attrition.

    3. State a prediction (direction + rationale):

      • e.g., Heritage < 2L1 in gender agreement accuracy but Heritage ≈ 2L1 in lexical access.

    4. Method & measures (one task, one primary DV + one control DV):

      • AJT (accuracy + RT), elicited production (error rates), SPR (region RTs), or eye-tracking (gaze/first-pass/total time).
      • Include covariates: current use, dominance, education, proficiency.

    5. Adjudication: What outcome would indicate incomplete acquisition vs. attrition vs. processing pressure?

    🧩 In-class application (Session 3, 45 min) #

    Case vignette (triad comparison)

    • Group A (2L1 Spanish-English): balanced early input; high current use both languages.
    • Group B (Heritage Spanish): early Spanish at home, schooling in English; low current Spanish use.
    • Group C (Adult L2 Spanish): English L1, late classroom exposure to Spanish; high motivation.

    Task: Object clitics and subject expression

    • Predict accuracy and RT orderings across the three groups.
    • Identify which pattern would falsify “incomplete acquisition” for Group B and instead support attrition or processing.
    • Propose one instructional focus per group (e.g., input enhancement for clitics; discourse-linked practice for subject expression).

    ✅ Self-check (answers hidden) #

    1. Give one strength and one vulnerability typically associated with Heritage grammars.
    1. How would you distinguish incomplete acquisition from attrition in study design?
    1. Name two covariates that must be controlled when comparing 2L1 vs. Heritage outcomes.
    1. Which domain is most likely to show group differences across contexts and why?
    1. Give one falsifiable prediction contrasting Adult L2 vs. 2L1 for gender agreement.

    📝 Weekly analytical note (due before Week 6) #

    In ~300 words, select one property and two groups (e.g., Heritage vs. Adult L2). (a) State a theory-based prediction, (b) specify one task + primary DV, (c) list two covariates you will control, and (d) say what outcome would imply incomplete acquisition vs. attrition vs. processing.
