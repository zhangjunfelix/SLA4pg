    Week 9 — Syntax–Semantics Interface: Meaning Composition, Telicity, and Scope #

    Anchor text: Slabakova (2016), Second Language Acquisition, Chapter 10.

    ⭐ Overview (for preview) #

    • How syntactic structure and semantic composition interact to yield meanings (event types, definiteness/specificity, scope).
    • Why L2 learners struggle with telicity/aspect, article semantics, and quantifier scope—and what this predicts for data patterns.
    • Minimal, testable designs for the interface: truth-value judgment, acceptability with controlled contexts, and online measures.

    🎯 Learning goals #

    By the end of this week you should be able to:

    1. Explain how event structure (state, activity, accomplishment, achievement) and object quantization affect telicity.
    2. Distinguish definiteness vs. specificity and predict article-use challenges for learners from article-less L1s.
    3. Describe scope ambiguities and derive L1-specific predictions for inverse scope in L2.
    4. Propose a compact experiment (materials, context, DV) that targets a syntax–semantics interface contrast.

    📖 Reading (do before Session 1) #

    • Required: Slabakova (2016), Ch. 10 (focus on: compositionality; event semantics & telicity; article semantics: definiteness/specificity/genericity; quantifiers & scope).
    • Optional: Revisit Ch. 8 (syntax diagnostics) and Ch. 3 (processing) for links to structure and time-course.

    Guiding questions:

    • Why does eat apples pattern differently from eat an apple or eat the apple in aspectual interpretation?
    • How can the same surface sentence have two truth conditions (scope ambiguity), and how would you test which reading a learner has?

    🧠 Key terms #

    compositionality; event structure (state/activity/accomplishment/achievement); telicity; quantization vs. cumulativity; perfective vs. imperfective; article semantics; definiteness; specificity; genericity; quantifiers; scope; inverse scope; distributive vs. collective; truth-value judgment task (TVJT).

    🔍 Core ideas #

    • Event composition & telicity: VP meaning arises from verb + object; quantized objects (e.g., an apple, three apples) tend to yield telic interpretations (bounded events), while mass/plural bare objects (apples, rice) favor atelic readings.
    • Articles & DP features: Languages package [±definite], [±specific], and generic readings differently. Learners from article-less L1s must map new features to forms; expect overuse/underuse of the/a and confusion in context-dependent readings.
    • Quantification & scope: Sentences with multiple quantifiers (e.g., A student read every book) can have surface (∃ > ∀) and inverse (∀ > ∃) readings. L1 grammar and processing preferences shape which readings L2 learners access.

    🗂️ Mini-lecture outline (Session 1, 45 min) #

    1) Event structure & telicity #

    • Key notes:
      • Accomplishments (bounded events) vs. activities (unbounded) depend on the internal argument: eat an apple (telic) vs. eat apples (atelic).
      • Adding measure phrases (drink a glass of water) or resultative structure increases boundedness.
      • Predictions: Learners may misinterpret aspect when the object’s quantization or measure is the only cue.
    • Class spark: Label a set of VPs (provided) as telic/atelic and justify by object type/measure.

    2) Article semantics: definiteness, specificity, generics #

    • Key notes:
      • Definiteness = unique, familiar referent (the book); specificity = speaker intends a particular but not necessarily familiar referent (a certain book).
      • Generics: The tiger/The tigers/Tigers are endangered—form–meaning mapping varies.
      • L2 predictions: Article-less L1s overuse the for specificity or familiarity, underuse a in novel but non-specific contexts; difficulty with generic readings.
    • Micro-demo: Two-sentence contexts that force definite vs. indefinite vs. specific readings; students pick the felicitous article.

    3) Quantifier scope & ambiguity resolution #

    • Key notes:
      • Scope options: A student read every book (∃ > ∀ vs. ∀ > ∃).
      • Diagnostics: picture verification, TVJT with explicit contexts, or continuation tasks.
      • L2 predictions: Preference for surface scope early; inverse scope access depends on L1, proficiency, and processing load.
    • Think–pair–share: Draft one context sentence that forces inverse scope for A reviewer read every paper, then test with a minimal prompt.

    Pacing: ~12 min per mini-topic + ~9 min discussion.

    🧪 Methods micro-lab (Session 2, 45 min) #

    Goal: Build a minimal, diagnostic task for one interface domain.

    Choose ONE and complete the template:

    • A. Telicity via object quantization

      • Materials: Triplets (telic vs. atelic), e.g., eat an apple / eat apples / eat three apples.
      • Task: TVJT with animated clips (bounded/unbounded); or acceptability with aspectual adverbs (in an hour vs. for an hour).
      • Prediction: Learners with article-less/mass-preference L1s will over-accept atelic + in an hour and under-accept telic + in an hour early on.

    • B. Article semantics (definite/specific/generic)

      • Materials: Two-sentence contexts fixing discourse status (unique/familiar, novel-specific, generic).
      • Task: Contexted AJT (choose the/a/∅), plus a short explanation prompt.
      • Prediction: Overuse of the in novel-specific contexts; instability in generic mappings (Tigers are… vs. The tiger is…).

    • C. Quantifier scope

      • Materials: Contexts that force inverse scope; test sentences with two quantifiers.
      • Task: TVJT with pictures/scenarios; optional SPR for online costs at disambiguation.
      • Prediction: Preference for surface scope; inverse scope improves with proficiency and supportive contexts.

    Deliverable: 3-line prereg—hypothesis, primary DV (accuracy/TVJ acceptances, RTs), and falsifier.

    🧩 In-class application (Session 3, 45 min) #

    Case vignette

    • Group A (Mandarin L1 → English): unstable article choices in novel-specific contexts; overuse of the.
    • Group B (Russian L1 → English): near-native telicity with measure phrases but difficulty with generic readings; surface-biased scope preferences.

    Questions:

    1. Which patterns suggest feature-mapping problems (DP) vs. composition problems (telicity) vs. processing preferences (scope)?
    2. What context manipulation would make the target meaning diagnostic for each group?
    3. Propose one classroom intervention (e.g., article choice with discourse cards; telicity training via in/for an hour diagnostics; scope paraphrase/verification drills).

    1. How can aspectual adverbs diagnose telicity?
