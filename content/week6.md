### 🧠 Key terms
*inflectional morphology; agreement; tense/aspect; case; gender; Missing Surface Inflection Hypothesis (MSIH); Prosodic Transfer Hypothesis (PTH); Feature Reassembly Hypothesis (FRH); realization vs. representation; mapping; optionality; comprehension–production asymmetry; priming.*

---

### 🔍 Core ideas
- **Variability ≠ ignorance**: Learners can **know** a feature (e.g., 3sg agreement) yet **omit** or mis-realize its **surface exponent** under pressure.
- **MSIH**: Representations are intact; variability is at the **spell-out**/phonological mapping stage → predicts better **comprehension** than **production**.
- **PTH**: L1 **prosodic templates** affect where/when morphology can surface → predicts **position-specific** or **syllable/foot**-sensitive omissions.
- **Feature Reassembly (FRH)**: L2 requires **reconfiguring** features and their **bundling** (e.g., definiteness, number, gender) → predicts **asymmetric success** across features and languages, even with the same “category label.”
- **Diagnostics**: Contrast **form-identical** vs. **feature-identical** items; compare **comprehension** (AJT, priming) vs. **production** (elicitation) to locate the bottleneck.

### 🗂️ Mini-lecture outline (Session 1, 45 min)

#### 1) What “lives” in morphology and why it’s hard
- **Key notes**: Agreement (person/number), tense/aspect, case, gender—small forms, big feature load; low perceptual salience; late-acquired and low-frequency combinations are especially vulnerable.
- **Class spark**: 2-minute poll—Which morphology caused you most trouble in an L2 (and in what context)?

#### 2) Three explanations for variability
- **MSIH (surface realization bottleneck)**  
  - **Prediction**: Near-native **interpretation** of tense/agreement contrasts; **omissions/mis-formations** in **production**—especially under speed/complexity.
- **PTH (prosodic constraints on realization)**  
  - **Prediction**: Omissions tied to **prosodic position** (e.g., word-final weak syllables) or clitic placement patterns; improvement when prosody favors overt marking.
- **FRH (feature (re)mapping)**  
  - **Prediction**: Difficulty where L2 requires **new feature bundles** or **different feature–form mappings** (e.g., gender on determiners/adjectives; article definiteness for bare-N languages).

#### 3) How to tell them apart (design heuristics)
- **Key notes**:  
  - **Comprehension vs. production** asymmetry → favors **MSIH**.  
  - **Position/syllable effects** → favors **PTH**.  
  - **Feature-specific cross-language asymmetries** (same form, different features) → favors **FRH**.  
- **Quick demo**: Judge vs. produce minimal pairs for 3sg -s, past -ed, or gender agreement; note where breakdown occurs.

> **Pacing**: Three focused mini-sections (~12 min each) + 9 min guided discussion.

### 🧪 Methods micro-lab (Session 2, 45 min)
**Goal**: Build a **discriminating test** that can separate MSIH, PTH, and FRH accounts.

1) **Pick one domain** (one only):  
   - (A) 3sg **agreement** in English, (B) **past tense** -ed, (C) **gender** agreement (Spanish), or (D) **case** marking (German).

2) **Contrast two tasks** (to locate the bottleneck):  
   - **Comprehension**: AJT with minimal pairs or morphological **priming** (facilitation when features match).  
   - **Production**: **Elicited production** under time pressure vs. no time pressure.

3) **Add a prosody manipulation** (if relevant):  
   - Strong vs. weak final syllables; clitic position; stress pattern changes.

4) **Predictions**:  
   - **MSIH** → intact comprehension, variable production, **bigger time-pressure effect**.  
   - **PTH** → position-sensitive omissions; prosody tweak reduces errors.  
   - **FRH** → selective difficulty tied to **feature bundles** (e.g., gender+number), even when prosody/time are controlled.

5) **Output**: 3-line prereg summary: hypothesis, primary DV (accuracy/RT/priming), and falsifier.

### 🧩 In-class application (Session 3, 45 min)

- **Group B (L1 English → L2 Spanish)**: good lexical gender knowledge but inconsistent **adjective agreement** and **determiner–noun** concord.

**Questions**:  
1) Which pattern points to **MSIH** vs. **FRH**, and why?
