    Second Language Acquisition (Postgraduate) #

    How do adults actually learn another language?
    This course is your guided tour through the science of SLA—linking linguistic theory to real data, real learners, and real classrooms.

    What this course is about #

    • We explore how language knowledge is organized (architecture), what varies across languages, and how the mind processes language in real time.
    • We zoom in on functional morphology, syntax, and the interfaces with semantics, discourse, and pragmatics.
    • We connect theory to teaching decisions you can defend with evidence.

    You’ll read selectively from Slabakova (2016) and a handful of research papers, then turn claims into testable predictions and compact study designs.

    Is this for me? #

    Yes if you want to…

    • move beyond “rules” to explanatory theories that make predictions,
    • become fluent in the methods used to test those predictions (from acceptability judgments to eye-tracking/ERP),
    • translate evidence into instructional choices that actually target learner bottlenecks.

    Some background in linguistics helps, but the course scaffolds everything you need.

    What you’ll be able to do #

    • Explain why certain L2 features are hard (and which ones),
    • Design a minimal experiment or classroom diagnostic to test a claim,
    • Read a study and separate competence from processing explanations,
    • Propose evidence-based interventions (e.g., focus-on-form for morphology).

    How the course runs #

    • 12 weeks × 3 sessions per week (45 minutes each).
    • A steady weekly rhythm:
      • Concept mini-lectures + quick demos
      • Methods micro-lab (turn claim → prediction → task)
      • Case application + teaching takeaways

    What we read #

    • Anchor text: Second Language Acquisition (Slabakova, 2016).
    • Plus targeted articles to deepen key topics (processing, interfaces, morphology) when needed.

    How to get started #

    • Skim the syllabus page to see the course logic and the “theory → prediction → test” workflow.
    • Bring one L2 phenomenon you’re curious about (e.g., articles, agreement, word order) — we’ll turn it into a researchable question.

    Tip: If you want a head start, think of a concrete learner difficulty you’ve seen. Be ready to say what would count as evidence for your explanation.
